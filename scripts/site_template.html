PSyGenTAB: Privacy-Constrained Synthetic Health Data at Sampling Time

Set a privacy floor. Release the most useful synthetic patient cohort that meets it — for any tabular generator, without retraining.

Overview

Privacy as a dial, not an afterthought

Synthetic patient data can unlock multi-site clinical research, but modern generators memorise their training patients, and differentially private training often destroys the clinical signal that made the data worth sharing. PSyGenTAB treats privacy as a configurable constraint at sampling time. A data custodian picks a privacy floor Pmin; PSyGenTAB draws candidate cohorts from an already-trained generator, scores every record against the training data and an unseen calibration holdout, and releases the most useful cohort whose holdout-calibrated privacy score meets the floor — solved exactly with an augmented Lagrangian. Because it only touches sampling, the same trained model can serve an internal team at a permissive floor and an external partner at a strict one.

Method

Constrained selection, solved at sampling time

PSyGenTAB never touches the generator's weights. It searches over what to release: a sampling configuration c (e.g. temperature) and a record-acceptance threshold τ.

Score proximity

Each candidate record s gets ρ(s): its distance to an equal-size subsample of the training data divided by its distance to the calibration holdout. ρ ≈ 1 means "no closer to training patients than to strangers".

Calibrate privacy

P combines three holdout-calibrated terms — the share of records closer to train than holdout (two-sided around ½), the NNDR ratio, and excess exact matches — so P=1 means indistinguishable from fresh real data.

Maximise utility

U averages marginal fidelity, association fidelity, a detection test and TSTR effectiveness, all against the calibration holdout.

Solve with ALM

Powell–Hestenes–Rockafellar augmented Lagrangian over the candidate set; multipliers grow until the floor holds. The selected release is then audited on the untouched test split.

maxc,τ  U(c,τ)   s.t.   P(c,τ) ≥ Pmin
ℒ = (1−U) + [max(0, λ + μg)² − λ²] / 2μ,   g = Pmin − P
PDCR = 1 − 2·| sharecloser-to-train − ½ |
P = ½·PDCR + ¼·min(1, NNDRsyn/NNDRhold) + ¼·(1 − excess exact-match)

Two-sided by design: pushing synthetic records away from training patients is itself a membership signal (Ganev & De Cristofaro, 2023). DP generators are released raw — filtering against training data would void their guarantee.

Interactive · fairness & diversity map

Does privacy filtering erase rare patients?

A distance filter removes the records that sit far from everyone else — exactly where minority subgroups live. We measure representation parity, worst-subgroup utility, rare-category coverage, tail preservation and more, per generator (mean over datasets, ± 1 SD across datasets).

Privacy audit

Attacked five ways

Every release is attacked by an adversary who knows its members are drawn from the training split and its non-members from the untouched test split. AUC 0.5 = the attacker learns nothing.

Distance MIA

Black-box: a record is called a member if it sits close to the synthetic data.

DOMIAS

Density-ratio attack (van Breugel et al., 2023): local over-density of synthetic data around a record, relative to a reference population.

Shadow-model MIA

TAPAS-style: the attacker retrains the generator 60 times with and without the most isolated patients and learns a per-target classifier.

Attribute inference

Anonymeter (Giomi et al., 2023): can the outcome of a known patient be inferred from the release, beyond a control baseline?

Linkability

Anonymeter: can two halves of a patient's record be linked through the synthetic data?

Shadow-model attack: the strongest adversary

The attacker retrains each generator on 60 worlds with and without the 5 most isolated (most vulnerable) training patients. Each cell shows how much PSyGenTAB changes the attacker's advantage |AUC − 0.5| for one generator on one dataset: green = leaks less, orange = leaks more. The right column gives the mean advantage, raw → PSyGenTAB. Click a row for the per-dataset detail.

An AUC below 0.5 means the attacker's guesses are systematically inverted. |AUC − 0.5| still counts it as leakage, which is conservative: in practice an attacker cannot know to flip their guesses without labelled data.

Reproducibility

Data, models and code

Every number on this page is produced by one resumable pipeline. Each run writes its released synthetic datasets, candidate tables, ALM traces, fitted model (GPU models) and a full evaluation JSON.

# environment (Python 3.10, pinned)
pip install -r requirements-study.txt

# CPU lane: BayesNet, ARF, TVAE, CTGAN, TabDDPM, PATE-GAN
python -m scripts.run_all --lane cpu --workers 3
# GPU lane: REaLTabFormer, CTAB-GAN+, DP-CTAB-GAN+
python -m scripts.run_all --lane gpu --workers 8 --gpus 0,1,2,3

# analysis
python -m scripts.tapas_attack --dataset heart_failure --generator rtf
python -m scripts.ablations && python -m scripts.aggregate
python -m scripts.figures   && python -m scripts.build_site
